Eight Decades in the Diamond Trade

1945

Kirtilal Laxmichand Jhaveri starts the family's diamond trading business from a small room in his house in Gamdevi, alongside partners Jormal and Ratilal Jhaveri.

Early 1960s

The firm moves into office space at Prasad Chambers, marking its transition from a home-run trading operation to an established presence in the trade.

1970 – 1985

The business expands beyond trading into manufacturing, building the foundation of expertise that would carry through generations.

2013

The 4th generation enters the business, training in diamond and jewellery manufacturing in Surat.

2015

Manufacturing begins independently, alongside buying from major global manufacturers to meet customer demand.

2025

Altair is founded, bringing eight decades of trade expertise into a single, customer-first jewellery house.

2026

Altair launches.

Altair started with a simple belief: that buying fine jewellery should feel like a collaboration, not a transaction.

Too often, the process is opaque. Stones graded in language designed to confuse, prices that bear no relationship to cost, and pieces that could belong to anyone. We set out to change that.

We believe in openness at every step. The stone, the setting, the price. Nothing is obscured. Through enduring relationships with the world's finest manufacturers, we offer jewellery of exceptional quality and craftsmanship. Every stone is hand-selected. Every setting is considered. The result is a jewel built around the person wearing it.

Founder's Note

Why Altair Exists.

I joined my family's business straight after NYU and spent the next decade in the B2B side of the diamond industry, getting stones polished and selling them to dealers. I was still one step removed from the people actually wearing them.

The turning point was when a woman came to us wanting to remake her wedding jewellery into an heirloom for her daughter. She'd believed for decades it was 18k gold set with fine white diamonds. When we tested it, it turned out to be gold-plated silver, set with low-quality brown diamonds.

We didn't want that happening to anyone else. That's why Altair exists. You'll get exactly what we've promised, or better. Never anything less.

— Aditya Mehta, Founder

From Stone to Setting

The Altair Guide
to Fine Jewellery.

We believe every buyer deserves to understand exactly what they are purchasing. This guide distils over eighty years of trade expertise into practical knowledge, so you can make decisions with confidence, not just trust.

The 4Cs are the universal language of diamond quality, standardised by the GIA. Understanding all four and how they interact is essential to buying well.

Cut
The most important C. Cut governs brilliance, fire, and scintillation. How a diamond moves in light. A well-cut stone will outperform a larger, poorly-cut one every time.

Every fancy shape is chosen for the best cut available at its price. What we check for changes with the shape. For an oval or pear, that means minimal to zero bow-tie. The goal stays the same: a stone that performs, not just measures well.
Colour
Graded D (colourless) to Z (light yellow). The higher the grade, the less colour the stone carries. For most settings, a near-colourless stone faces up white and is visually indistinguishable from the top grades.
Clarity
Graded from Internally Flawless to I3. The practical benchmark: eye-clean. A stone where inclusions are invisible to the unaided eye performs identically to a flawless stone in daily wear.
Carat
Weight, not size. Two diamonds of identical carat can look very different depending on cut proportions. Always evaluate spread alongside carat weight.

Every diamond cut carries its own character. These are the cuts we return to most at Altair.

Round Brilliant

58 facets engineered for maximum light return. The benchmark against which all other cuts are measured.

Oval

All the brilliance of a round, elongated. Appears larger than its carat weight. One of our most requested cuts.

Radiant

Cropped corners with brilliant faceting. Combines the clean lines of an emerald cut with the fire of a round. Exceptional for fancy yellows.

Long Cushion

Soft corners, brilliant facets. Romantic without being delicate. Sits beautifully as a centre stone.

Pear

Pointed at one end, rounded at the other. Directional and distinctive. The point should always face away from the hand when set in a ring.

Marquise

The longest-looking cut relative to carat weight. Sharp at both ends, commanding in a solitaire setting.

Trilliant

Three-sided, brilliant-faceted. Most often used as an accent flanking a centre stone, though striking as a solitaire.

Rose Cut

A flat base and domed top with facets that create a gentle, diffused light. Soft and warm, quite unlike the sharp brilliance of a brilliant cut.

Lab-grown diamonds are physically, chemically, and optically identical to mined diamonds. The only difference is origin and therefore price. Both are graded by the same gemological laboratories on the same 4C criteria.

At Altair, we offer both with full transparency. Our Atelier line uses natural diamonds sourced to the highest standards. Our Studio line uses lab-grown diamonds held to the same quality benchmark, at a fraction of the cost.

The right choice depends on what matters most to you: the geological rarity of a natural stone, or the ability to allocate more budget to cut quality and size with a lab-grown equivalent.

A diamond certificate is a third-party, objective grading report.

GIA is the oldest and most conservative grader, often considered the industry standard for natural diamonds. IGI is widely used for lab-grown stones and graded consistently with GIA's standards. At Altair, IGI certification is available on request.

When reviewing a certificate, look closely at the cut grade (for rounds), the clarity plot (the map of inclusions), and fluorescence. Faint to medium fluorescence is generally neutral and can sometimes enhance the appearance of lower-colour stones.

The setting holds and presents the diamond. The choice of setting style dramatically affects how a stone looks, how much light it receives, and how the piece wears over time.

Prong
Maximum light exposure. Classic and versatile. Our default at Altair.
Bezel
Metal surrounds the stone completely. Clean, architectural look. More protective. Slightly reduces brilliance.
Pavé
Small accent diamonds set flush across the band or shoulders. Continuous diamond surface, maximum light from multiple points.
Channel
Stones set between two parallel metal rails. Sleek and secure. No prongs, no snag risk.

Diamonds are the hardest natural substance, but settings are not. Routine care ensures your piece retains its brilliance and structural integrity indefinitely.

Clean with warm water, a drop of mild dish soap, and a soft toothbrush. Avoid ultrasonic cleaners for pavé settings. Vibration can loosen smaller stones. Have prongs checked annually by a jeweller.

Store each piece separately in a soft pouch or box to prevent surface contact. Remove before swimming, exercising, or applying perfume. Chlorine and oils are the primary enemies of gold alloys.

All Altair pieces come with a complimentary annual check-up at our Mumbai studio.
